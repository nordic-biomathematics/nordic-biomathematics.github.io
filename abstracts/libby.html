Multicellular information and the regulation of early life cycles

Eric Libby, Umeå University, Sweden

A key step in the evolution of complex multicellularity is the emergence of developmental regulation, coordinating cellular division and multicellular reproduction across a life cycle. While prior work has explored the role of external factors such as the environment in shaping multicellular life cycles, little is known about the capacity for selection to harness cell-level information for novel developmental regulation. Here, I will discuss models of simple forms of multicellular organization and how various types of cell-level information (e.g., cell age and mechanical stress) can be co-opted to produce regular multicellular life cycles. One main finding is that these sources of cell-level information fail to generate specific life cycles, lacking either flexibility or regularity. Even when deterministic rules are used to regulate multicellular reproduction, some types of information lead to a plurality of life cycles, with wide variation in reproductive mode and tempo. We do find that when multiple sources of information are combined in simple genetic circuits, this can reduce the variance in life cycles, but only when the initial single sources of information were deficient. Together these results suggest that, while multicellular evolution may be facilitated by co-opting cell-level information, there are significant limitations on the degree to which this information can be harnessed to evolve a tightly-regulated life cycle. Our work highlights the constraints faced by early multicellular organisms, and the evolutionary innovations required for the emergence of coordinated multicellular development.
