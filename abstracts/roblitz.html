A Mathematical Perspective on Endocrine Regulation

Susanna Röblitz, University of Bergen, Norway

Hormones enable communication between organs and tissues and regulate essential physiological processes such as metabolism, stress response, and reproduction. Many endocrine processes exhibit oscillatory dynamics across timescales ranging from milliseconds to days, which has important implications for understanding disease and optimizing drug therapy. However, the complexity of endocrine regulation, combined with limited and imperfect data, makes the development of quantitative models particularly challenging. In this presentation, I will discuss our work on mathematical modelling of hormonal dynamics, with a focus on the female menstrual cycle and its interaction with other hormonal axes. The talk will highlight the importance of fit-for-purpose modelling and the potential of such approaches to support translational research.
