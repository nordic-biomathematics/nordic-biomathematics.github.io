Constraint-based explanations in mathematical biology

David Sumpter, Uppsala University, Sweden

Over a series of articles, Sara Green has shown how most explanations in biology fall into two primary families: dynamic mechanistic explanations, which say how a system works by naming its parts and their organisation, and constraint-based explanations, which say what a whole class of systems can and cannot do, whatever their parts are made of. I will focus on the second type of explanation in mathematical biology, showing how to think of models such as Fisher's sex ratio, Vicsek's model for collective motion, the central limit theorem and the Poincaré–Bendixson theorem in terms of constraints. Green also provides a template for classifying an explanation as either mechanistic or constraint-based. I show how framing explanations using these templates can help clear up, sometimes heated, debates around inclusive fitness, collective motion and other aspects of group behavior in animals. I further argue that new constraints can be found by just thinking about something hard enough, encouraging us to rediscover (what Gyllingberg et al. call) the lost art of creating new mathematical models.
